## Assumption 1 — Technical
### "The AI can interpret CXL debug data well enough to provide useful diagnoses"

**What must be true:** An LLM or fine-tuned ML model can ingest CXL link training logs, register dumps, and protocol traces — including data generated by real silicon from specific vendors — and produce root-cause suggestions that are more useful than what a validation engineer would derive manually.

**Fragility: HIGH**

**Evidence for fragility:**

**No published research has demonstrated LLMs working on post-silicon protocol-layer debug.** Every study in the LLM-for-EDA literature operates on pre-silicon artifacts (Verilog RTL, simulation logs). A 2025 survey of 91% of published LLM-EDA studies found four application categories (code generation, verification/debug, knowledge retrieval, optimization), and post-silicon protocol debug appears in none of them. The academic papers closest to this space — DAC 2025 Article No. 30 ("LLMs reshaping the role of AI in post-silicon test engineering") and IEEE VTS 2025 special session — are research proposals, not deployed systems. Sources: [LLMs for EDA Survey (arXiv 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20030v1), [DAC 2025 proceedings](https://dl.acm.org/doi/proceedings/10.5555/3778334), [VTS 2025 program](https://tttc-vts.org/public_html/new/2025/program-3/index.html)

**The most diagnostically useful data is precisely the least accessible.** CXL debug requires five distinct data modalities: (a) LTSSM state machine traces, (b) standard register dumps, (c) FLIT-level protocol analyzer captures, (d) analog signal integrity measurements (eye diagrams, PAM4 jitter), and (e) vendor-proprietary PHY debug registers. Of these, the last two are either uninterpretable by text-based LLMs or locked behind NDA. Vendor debug registers from Astera Labs Aries, Montage Technology, and XConn controllers capture the internal state most diagnostic of link training failure — and none of it is in any training corpus. Source: [Technical Feasibility Research, this report; Synopsys CXL Memory-Mapped Registers blog](https://www.synopsys.com/blogs/chip-design/access-memory-mapped-registers-cxl.html)

**The public CXL specification corpus is insufficient for reliable fine-tuning.** Five CXL spec versions (1.0–3.2) at ~2,000 pages each yields roughly 5 million tokens of domain text. Nvidia's ChipNeMo required 24 billion tokens of internal chip design data to meaningfully outperform base LLaMA2-70B on *generic* chip design tasks — not CXL-specific ones. A CXL-specific fine-tuned model would need orders of magnitude more domain text than exists publicly. Source: [ChipNeMo (Nvidia Research)](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2023-10_chipnemo-domain-adapted-llms-chip-design); [ChipNeMo arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.00176)

**Hallucination on vendor-specific behavior is structurally unavoidable.** CXL link training debug requires knowing how a *specific vendor's* PHY firmware behaves under a *specific* failure condition. LLMs cannot learn this from public sources because this data doesn't exist publicly. The result is "confident wrong answers" — an LLM may suggest checking a register field that doesn't exist in a given implementation. In post-silicon debug, a plausible-but-incorrect diagnosis costs days or weeks of misdirected engineering. Source: [LLMs and the Future of Chip Design: Security Risks (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07061v1)

**Analog signal interpretation is architecturally impossible for text LLMs.** CXL 3.x link training at 32 GT/s (PAM4) requires interpreting eye diagrams, jitter spectra, and equalization parameter decisions. Text LLMs cannot process oscilloscope captures. Adding a vision model requires a separate ML pipeline with its own training data scarcity problem. Source: [Technical Feasibility Research, this report; Xilinx PCIe Debug K-Map](https://xilinx.github.io/pcie-debug-kmap/pciedebug/build/html/docs/Link_Training/general_debug_checklist_reasons_questions.html)

**Kill scenario:** The AI component ships as a RAG-based assistant over public specs. It answers questions about what the spec *says* but cannot diagnose what a specific device *is doing*. Validation engineers use it for one week, encounter a novel failure mode that the model addresses with a confident but wrong diagnosis, spend three days chasing the wrong root cause, and conclude the tool is actively harmful. Word spreads in the CXL validation community — a small, tight-knit world — and subsequent pilots cannot get past the initial technical evaluation.

**Mitigation (60-day experiment):** Build a minimal RAG demo over the public CXL 3.1 spec and run it against 20 "known-answer" questions derived from actual link training failure cases contributed by two validation engineers at a pilot partner. Measure: (a) hallucination rate on vendor-specific behavior questions, (b) whether spec-grounded answers alone would have accelerated the real debug cycle, (c) whether engineers trust the outputs enough to act on them. If hallucination rate exceeds 20% on vendor-specific questions, the technical assumption is demonstrably fragile at this stage.

## Assumption 2 — Data
### "Enough labeled CXL debug cases exist to train or fine-tune a useful model"

**What must be true:** The company can assemble a training dataset of sufficient volume and quality — failures paired with verified root causes and raw log artifacts — to train a model that generalizes beyond known failure patterns to novel ones.

**Fragility: HIGH** (the single most structurally dangerous assumption)

**Evidence for fragility:**

**CXL link training failures are rare by design.** Silicon that fails link training during pre-silicon verification doesn't tape out. The corpus of post-silicon CXL failures at any single company is measured in the tens to low hundreds of events per product generation — not the thousands or millions needed for reliable supervised learning. Based on industry data, a single CXL silicon team in 2026 is unlikely to have more than 50–200 distinct root-cause-labeled failure events in their history. Source: [Technical Feasibility Research, this report; ML for Post-Silicon Chip Validation (Medium)](https://medium.com/@preethishnananbotlagunta/machine-learning-models-for-accelerating-post-silicon-chip-validation-265226dc75fe)

**Cross-company data pooling is blocked by IP risk.** The most useful training data — proprietary trace captures, PHY debug register dumps, root-cause analysis reports — is also the most sensitive design IP in the industry. Semiconductor companies will not share this with a third-party AI platform unless deployment is fully on-premise, which eliminates the data flywheel (more customers → more shared data → better model). The IP sensitivity culture in semiconductor is extreme: any tool that ingests design data triggers a full legal, IT security, and export control review. Source: [Semiconductor GTM Research, this report; Seclore Semiconductor IP Protection](https://www.seclore.com/why-seclore/semiconductor/); [IP Protection for Semiconductor Startups (Siemens)](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/cre8ventures/2024/11/11/strategies-on-ip-protection-for-semiconductor-startups/)

**Labeling is slow, expensive, and requires senior engineers.** Each failure needs ground-truth root-cause labeling. Labeling a single CXL link training failure — involving correlation across LTSSM logs, register dumps, analog SI data, and vendor firmware behavior — requires a senior validation engineer spending 2–8 hours per event. At 200 failures × 5 hours = 1,000 person-hours minimum for a single company's historical corpus. And this assumes the root cause was definitively established at the time of the failure, which is often not true for intermittent or non-reproducible failures. Source: [CXL Debug Pain Points Research, this report; Caliber Interconnects post-silicon validation](https://www.caliberinterconnect.com/2025/06/06/post-silicon-validation-in-advanced-soc-development-a-comprehensive-technical-overview/)

**No standard format exists for CXL debug data.** Teledyne LeCroy Summit M616, VIAVI Xgig 6P16, Keysight CXL 3 analyzers, and SerialTek Kodiak all capture CXL traces in proprietary binary formats. Building ingestion pipelines for all of them is a 6–12 month engineering project. Without standardized ingestion, training data from multiple customers cannot be combined. Source: [Competitive Landscape Research, this report; SerialTek Kodiak](https://serialtek.com/kodiak/)

**The catch-22 that services-first creates for data.** If the GTM strategy is services (the company's engineers do the debug, not the AI), the data generated during service engagements is the customer's IP. The customer has legitimate grounds to claim that AI models trained on their trace logs and failure analyses are derived from their proprietary work. This has been identified as an IP ownership risk that kills the AI product flywheel in adjacent markets. Source: [Semiconductor GTM Research, this report]

**Kill scenario:** After 18 months of services engagements with 3–4 pilot customers, the company's "training dataset" consists of 150 labeled failures from 2 companies who gave partial permission to use their data, and 100 internally-generated synthetic failures that don't match real silicon behavior. The model performs well on the 40 failure types it has seen but fails on the first novel failure mode encountered in a pilot at a new customer. The customer concludes the AI is not ready and chooses to continue manual debug. The company is trapped: it cannot improve the model without more data, and it cannot get more data without proving the model works.

**Mitigation (60-day experiment):** Do a data audit with one pilot partner before writing a line of model code. Ask them to open their debug issue tracker and count: (a) how many CXL-specific link training failures from the past 24 months have documented root causes, (b) how many have associated raw log artifacts still available, and (c) whether they would license those artifacts for model training under an NDA. If the answer is fewer than 500 labeled failures with artifacts, and they will not permit model training, the data assumption is falsified before any engineering investment is made. This experiment costs one engineering week and one sales relationship call.

## Assumption 3 — Market Timing
### "CXL adoption is at the right inflection point to create a market now"

**What must be true:** Enough CXL silicon is in active post-silicon validation cycles at enough companies to create a repeatable TAM — not just a handful of hyperscalers doing one-off internal work.

**Fragility: MEDIUM**

**Evidence for fragility:**

**CXL production deployments remain narrow in 2026.** Despite significant ecosystem activity (35+ sessions at CXL DevCon 2025, Keysight launching CXL 3 validation solutions at DesignCon 2026, CXL 4.0 spec released November 2025 at 128 GT/s via PCIe 7.0), production CXL deployments as of Q1 2026 are concentrated at: a small number of hyperscalers (primarily Meta AI Research, Microsoft Azure AI, some AWS configurations), and CXL controller silicon vendors (Astera Labs, Montage Technology, XConn, Marvell). The broader enterprise server market has not yet standardized on CXL memory pooling. Source: [Competitive Landscape Research, this report; OCP Summit 2025](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/10/13/data-infrastructure/ocp-summit-2025-the-open-future-of-networking-hardware-for-ai/)

**The world's largest AI compute buyers use proprietary interconnects that bypass CXL.** Google TPU v7 "Ironwood" (GA November 2025) uses a dual-chiplet design with Google's proprietary ICI (Inter-Chip Interconnect) — not CXL. Amazon Trainium and Inferentia use custom Neuron interconnects. Meta MTIA uses OCP-based designs. These hyperscalers are building the most AI compute in the world and are doing it *without CXL*. The CXL addressable market for post-silicon debug is primarily the silicon vendors (Astera Labs, Montage, XConn) and the hyperscalers building *memory expansion* systems, not AI training accelerators. Sources: [Google Ironwood TPU v7 (Google Research)](https://blog.google/technology/ai/2025-research-breakthroughs/); [Meta MTIA (Meta, March 2026)](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/expanding-metas-custom-silicon-to-power-our-ai-workloads/)

**The debug market size is a function of the number of tapeouts, not deployments.** For a post-silicon debug tool, what matters is not production volume but the number of distinct validation cycles happening per year. CXL silicon tapeouts in 2025–2026 are primarily at: Astera Labs (Leo series), Montage Technology (memory interconnect controllers), XConn (CXL switches), Marvell (CXL retimers), and hyperscaler custom silicon (Maia 200, MTIA 4/5). This is a small number of distinct validation teams — plausibly 15–30 globally in 2026. At $100–300K per tool license, the immediate TAM is ~$3–9M ARR — not enough to build a standalone company without significant expansion to PCIe broadly. Sources: [DesignCon 2026 Keysight](https://www.keysight.com/us/en/about/newsroom/news-releases/2026/0218-pr-026-keysight-introduces-scale-up-validation-solutions-for-ai-data-centers.html); CXL Protocol Analyzer Market report at [dataintelo.com](https://dataintelo.com/report/cxl-protocol-analyzer-market/amp)

**Evidence that timing is favorable (countering arguments):**

CXL adoption is accelerating in the 2025–2026 window: MemVerge and XConn announced CXL memory pooling deployments at OCP Summit 2025; Samsung and SK Hynix have shipping CXL memory modules; the Linux kernel is actively adding CXL RAS support (Linux 6.16 with CXL Patrol Scrub, Error Check Scrub — active development confirms deployed silicon creating real failures). The fact that Keysight launched CXL 3 protocol validation tools at DesignCon 2026 and the CXL Consortium has active working groups at every major conference suggests that the validation problem is real and growing. Source: [Phoronix Linux 6.16 CXL](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Linux-6.16-CXL); [OCP Summit 2025](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/10/13/data-infrastructure/ocp-summit-2025-the-open-future-of-networking-hardware-for-ai/)

**Kill scenario:** CXL memory pooling adoption in AI data centers stalls because (a) PCIe 6.0 (64 GT/s) without CXL is sufficient for the bandwidth requirements of current AI training interconnects, (b) hyperscalers decide their proprietary interconnects outcompete CXL for latency-sensitive workloads, and (c) the broader enterprise server market won't adopt CXL at scale until 2028+. The company finds itself with 5–8 potential customers globally, all of whom are doing evaluation rather than production deployment, creating a $1–2M ARR ceiling rather than a scalable business.

**Mitigation (60-day experiment):** Map every CXL silicon tapeout announced between 2024 and 2026 (using public CXL Consortium member list, DesignCon presentations, OCP working group membership, job postings). Count the number of distinct validation teams worldwide. If the count is below 20 active teams in 2026 expanding to below 50 by 2027, the TAM is too narrow for a standalone product company and the GTM must expand to PCIe 6.0 validation immediately, not CXL-first. Sources: [CXL Consortium DevCon 2025](https://computeexpresslink.org/blog/teledyne-lecroy-to-demonstrate-protocol-analyzer-and-protocol-exerciser-for-cxl-3-x-at-devcon-2025-3829/)

## Assumption 4 — GTM
### "A services-first model will convert to product revenue"

**What must be true:** Revenue from custom debug engagements (time-and-materials consulting, bespoke AI model development for specific customers) will create the relationships, proof points, and product insight needed to transition customers to an annual software license.

**Fragility: HIGH**

**Evidence for fragility:**

**The semiconductor sales cycle makes 18-month runways structurally insufficient for product conversion.** Based on five completed historical case studies (Tortuga Logic, Metrics Design Automation, Jasper Design Automation, OneSpin, Verific), every EDA startup that achieved meaningful commercial traction took 3–7 years. The fastest path — Metrics, using a former Cadence CEO's personal network — still required 12–18 months to first enterprise contract. For a team without that founder network, the realistic timeline from first customer contact to signed annual software license at a hyperscaler is 18–36 months, with 13 months as the absolute floor. Source: [Semiconductor GTM Research, this report; Enterprise sales cycle: HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/enterprise-sales-cycle)

**Services creates the wrong organizational relationship for a product sale.** When a startup provides custom debug services, the value perception at the customer is "we hired smart engineers who helped us." The transition to "we are licensing software that automates this" requires a completely different buying conversation with a different buyer persona (software procurement vs. engineering services procurement), a different economic frame (annual subscription vs. time-and-materials), and a product that demonstrably works *without* the startup's engineers present. Jasper Design Automation took 15 years from founding to Cadence acquisition precisely because the services-then-product transition is non-trivial. Source: [Semiconductor GTM Research, this report; Jasper acquisition](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/cadence-completes-acquisition-of-jasper-design-automation-263272671.html)

**IP ownership of AI models trained on customer data is legally ambiguous and a deal-killer.** If the first services engagements involve the startup ingesting a customer's proprietary CXL trace logs and building a custom AI model for their specific device, the customer has legitimate grounds to claim the resulting model weights are work product derived from their proprietary data. This directly blocks the model reuse that makes the AI product scalable. Multiple AI startups in adjacent markets (AI-powered code review, AI-powered test generation) have encountered this problem. Semiconductor companies have especially aggressive IP ownership postures. Source: [Semiconductor GTM Research, this report; Seclore Semiconductor IP](https://www.seclore.com/why-seclore/semiconductor/)

**SOC 2 Type II is a procurement prerequisite that cannot be rushed.** For any tool processing semiconductor design data, large customers require SOC 2 Type II certification (12-month observation period minimum). A services engagement that processes customer trace logs exposes the company to security questionnaires that cannot be satisfied without the certification. Beginning SOC 2 audit preparation on day one of the company means the certificate cannot arrive until month 12–18, blocking all Tier-1 contracts regardless of how good the services work is. Source: [Semiconductor GTM Research, this report; Vendor Security Review (UpGuard)](https://www.upguard.com/blog/vendor-security-review)

**The "services trap" feedback loop.** Customers conditioned to receive expert services will demand them as a condition of any license. If the first three engagements are funded by human debug work, the fourth customer will expect the same model. The product becomes a "tool + expert support bundle" at a custom price, never achieving the unit economics of a scalable software business. This was identified as the defining failure mode for EDA startups at the services-to-product transition point. Source: [EDA 3.0: EE Times](https://www.eetimes.com/eda-3-0-so-you-are-an-eda-startup/); [Semiconductor GTM Research, this report]

**Evidence for services-first (countering arguments):**

Jasper Design Automation successfully used "methodology consulting and turnkey proof kit development" as a wedge. Tortuga Logic used DARPA-funded services as first revenue before commercial product. A services engagement under $50–100K can bypass full procurement chains at many companies. Source: [Semiconductor GTM Research, this report; Jasper acquisition PR](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/cadence-completes-acquisition-of-jasper-design-automation-263272671.html)

**Kill scenario:** At 18 months, the company has three services customers paying $150–200K each for custom debug engagements. Investors note that gross margins are 30% (people-intensive), the "product" has no customers using it without the startup's engineers, and the model cannot be retrained on customer data without IP risk. Series B investors decline to fund on these metrics. The company is generating revenue but cannot raise growth capital because the services revenue pattern signals "consulting company" not "software company." The runway runs out before the product is proven to work autonomously.

**Mitigation (60-day experiment):** Structure the first pilot engagement from day one as a bounded PoC ($75–150K fixed fee) with explicitly pre-defined success criteria: "At the end of 90 days, [customer's engineers] will evaluate whether the software tool — running without our team present — reduces their CXL bring-up debug time by X%." Include a clause: "If success criteria are met, customer commits to a 12-month software license at $Y; if not, engagement ends." Track whether customers will sign this structure. If they refuse the software conversion clause, that is direct evidence the services-to-product assumption is false for this customer segment.

## Assumption 5 — Team / Moat
### "Domain expertise is defensible against larger incumbents"

**What must be true:** The founding team's CXL protocol knowledge and early customer relationships create a moat that cannot be rapidly replicated by Synopsys, Cadence, Keysight, or a well-funded acqui-hire.

**Fragility: MEDIUM-HIGH**

**Evidence for fragility:**

**Synopsys already has every structural advantage for this market except execution focus.** Synopsys has: (a) the CXL 4.0 Verification IP — the most complete CXL protocol corpus in the industry; (b) Verdi Protocol Analyzer with CXL protocol decode already integrated; (c) existing relationships with every CXL silicon vendor; (d) the largest EDA field sales force in the industry. Adding an AI "copilot" feature to Verdi Protocol Analyzer — powered by Verdi's existing CXL trace analysis capabilities — is a product extension decision, not a ground-up build. If the startup achieves early traction and Synopsys perceives a threat, the response time is 6–12 months to a competing feature, not 2–3 years. Source: [Competitive Landscape Research, this report; Synopsys CXL 4.0 VIP blog](https://www.synopsys.com/blogs/chip-design/industry-1stcxl-verification-ip-transforming-aihpc-systems.html)

**Cadence demonstrated the acquisition-of-AI-startup playbook with ChipStack (November 2025).** Cadence acquired ChipStack in November 2025 and released ChipStack AI Super Agent in February 2026 — a 3-month turnaround from acquisition to product launch. The same playbook is repeatable: observe a startup gaining traction in a gap, acquire before Series B pricing makes it expensive, integrate into existing platform. This is not hypothetical — it happened in the adjacent LLM-for-EDA space within the past 6 months. Source: [Cadence ChipStack press release, February 2026](https://www.cadence.com/en_US/home/company/newsroom/press-releases/pr/2026/cadence-unleashes-chipstack-ai-super-agent-pioneering-a-new.html); [HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/2026/02/12/cadence-introduces-agentic-ai-system-for-chip-design-and-verification/)

**Teledyne LeCroy and Keysight have the hardware moat but lack AI.** These companies already have the trust relationships with CXL validation labs, the protocol-layer capture hardware, and the CXL Consortium certifications. Adding an AI software layer to their existing products requires software investment, not hardware development. Keysight's DesignCon 2026 launch of Scale-Up Validation Solutions for AI Data Centers signals active investment in this exact space. If an AI software layer is the missing piece, a hardware company with existing customer relationships can add it faster than a software startup can build the hardware relationships. Source: [Keysight DesignCon 2026](https://www.keysight.com/us/en/about/newsroom/news-releases/2026/0218-pr-026-keysight-introduces-scale-up-validation-solutions-for-ai-data-centers.html)

**Evidence that domain expertise creates a real moat (countering arguments):**

All major EDA incumbents have confirmed their AI features are pre-silicon-centric and have explicitly stated this is their focus. Synopsys's AgentEngineer (March 2026), Cadence's ChipStack (February 2026), Siemens's Questa One (June 2025) — none of these products address post-silicon CXL protocol debug. The gap has been real for at least 18 months and no incumbent has moved to fill it. This suggests the incumbents are either unaware of the opportunity, deprioritizing it, or structurally unable to pivot their pre-silicon product teams to post-silicon problems. A startup that builds the first trusted AI post-silicon debug tool can establish a data flywheel (more customer failure data → better model → more customers) that becomes hard to replicate once the labeled dataset is large enough. Source: [Competitive Landscape Research, this report]

**The real moat is the data flywheel, not the domain knowledge.** Domain knowledge (understanding CXL protocol state machines, LTSSM debug procedures) can be hired. What cannot be hired is a proprietary dataset of thousands of labeled CXL link training failures paired with verified root causes across multiple silicon generations and vendors. If the company can assemble this dataset before incumbents recognize the opportunity, the model trained on it becomes genuinely defensible — because the training data is the product, and it can only be accumulated through trust-based customer relationships over years. The window to build this moat is approximately 18–24 months before Synopsys or Cadence can acquire it away. Source: [Competitive Landscape Research, this report; ChipNeMo data advantage analysis]

**Kill scenario:** The startup achieves 4–5 pilot customers and is working toward a Series A. Synopsys announces "Verdi AI Debug" — an AI-powered CXL trace analysis copilot integrated into Verdi Protocol Analyzer, powered by the CXL VIP corpus and Synopsys's existing customer relationships. Customers who were in evaluation pause and wait for the Synopsys product. The startup cannot compete on distribution, existing customer trust, or protocol corpus depth. It can compete on specialization and product velocity, but the window to do so is measured in months, not years.

**Mitigation (60-day experiment):** Map Synopsys and Cadence job postings for "post-silicon," "protocol debug," and "CXL validation" engineering roles. If either company is hiring specifically for these functions (not pre-silicon verification), that is a leading indicator of product investment. Also: interview 3 CXL validation engineers who work at companies that have both a commercial EDA relationship with Synopsys/Cadence AND an active CXL bring-up project. Ask: "Would you pilot a startup's AI debug tool if Synopsys announced they were building the same thing?" The answer determines whether the window is open or closing.

## Conclusion: Which Assumption Kills Fastest?

**The data assumption (Assumption 2) kills fastest, and it kills the entire company — not just one dimension.**

Here is the kill chain:

1. **Insufficient labeled training data** means the AI component cannot generalize beyond known failure patterns.
2. **AI that doesn't generalize** means the first novel failure a customer encounters results in a confident-but-wrong diagnosis.
3. **A confident-but-wrong diagnosis** in a CXL validation lab destroys trust immediately — validation engineers are precisely the people who will notice when an AI recommendation is wrong, and they will not forgive it.
4. **Lost trust from the AI** reverts the product to a "smart search tool over CXL specs" — useful, but easily replicated by any RAG deployment over public documents.
5. **A product that is just spec Q&A** has no defensible differentiation against Synopsys, Cadence, or an LLM wrapper built by the customers themselves.
6. **No defensible AI product** means services is the only revenue — which triggers the services trap (Assumption 4), which blocks the product transition.
7. **No product transition** means no scalable revenue, which means no Series B, which means company failure within 24–36 months.

The other assumptions can be partially mitigated: the technical limitation can be scoped down to a narrower AI use case (spec Q&A, known-failure pattern matching) that is honest about its limitations; the market timing assumption has enough positive evidence to proceed cautiously; the moat can be built if the data flywheel starts; the GTM can be restructured with paid PoC contracts that force the services-to-product decision early. **None of these mitigations work if the data assumption is false, because the AI product that justifies the entire company's existence cannot be built.**

## The Cheapest 60-Day Experiment

**Cost:** 1 engineer week + 1 sales call + 1 legal/NDA.

**What to do:** Before writing a line of model code, approach one target pilot customer (a CXL silicon vendor or hyperscaler memory team) and ask for a "data audit." Specifically:

1. Open their CXL debug issue tracker and count the number of link training failures from the past 24 months that have: (a) a documented root cause, AND (b) preserved raw log artifacts (LTSSM traces, register dumps, protocol analyzer captures).
2. Ask: "Would you license these artifacts to us under an NDA for AI model training, with no residual rights claimed by us to models trained on your data?"
3. If the answer to (1) is fewer than 500 labeled events and the answer to (2) is "no" — the data assumption is falsified before any engineering investment.

**If the experiment returns < 500 labeled failures across your first 3 pilot partners and none will share data for training:** The company is not building an AI debug product. It is building a knowledge-base search tool and an expert consulting service. Both can be valuable, but neither justifies AI startup valuations or a venture-scale return. The honest pivot is: (a) build the spec Q&A tool as a freemium wedge, (b) fund data collection through a DARPA/government research contract on CXL reliability, (c) price the services engagements as a data acquisition cost until the corpus is large enough to support model training. The business plan should be rewritten around the 36-month horizon to first reliable AI product, not the 18-month horizon that assumes the data already exists.
